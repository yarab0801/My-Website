💧 Voltage 101

Voltage is electrical pressure. It's the push that wants to shove electrons around. More pressure, more push. 💪

The water-pipe analogy

  • Voltage = water pressure (how hard it pushes)
  • Current = how much water flows per second
  • Voltage is always a difference. It's measured between two points, like "9 V higher than ground".
  • Ground (0 V) = the floor we measure everything from. 🧹

Meet the three main characters

I slow things down!

Rita the Resistor

A narrow pipe. She makes it harder for current to flow and "uses up" some voltage while doing it.

V = I × R

I hold the charge! 💖

Cappy the Capacitor

A little water tank. He stores charge, and his voltage tells you how full he is. He hates sudden voltage changes.

Q = C × V

Don't rush me! 🌀

Indy the Inductor

A heavy paddle wheel in the pipe. Once the water is moving, he keeps it moving. He hates sudden current changes.

V = L × dI/dt

🔋 The RC Circuit

Rita and Cappy hold hands in a loop with a battery. Cappy fills up... but only as fast as Rita lets him.

A = battery +  •  B = between Rita and Cappy  •  C = ground

The tank & the narrow pipe 🚰

Cappy is a water tank. The battery pushes water in through Rita, a narrow pipe.

At first the tank is empty, so there's a big pressure difference and water rushes in. As it fills, the pressure difference shrinks and the flow slows down. That's why it never fills at a steady speed. 🐢

The two always share the battery's voltage: Vs = Vr + Vc

τ = R × C

Vc = Vs × (1 − e−t/RC)

Vr = Vs × e−t/RC

τ is "tau", the circuit's time constant. Big R or big C = a slower circuit.

🎛️ Play with it!

Charge connects the battery to A. Discharge swaps the battery for a plain wire to ground, so A drops to 0 V and Cappy empties through Rita (her voltage goes negative because the current flips direction!).

A (battery +) B = Vc (across capacitor) Vr (across resistor, A − B)

📏 Rule of thumb

  • After 1 τ Cappy is about 63% full.
  • After 2 τ ≈ 86%, after 3 τ ≈ 95%.
  • After 5 τ he's basically full (99.3%). 🎉
  • Discharging works backwards: after 1 τ he's still holding about 37%.

🎢 The LC Circuit

Cappy starts out full. Indy is calm. Then things get bouncy!

A = top wire (the voltage on both parts)  •  C = ground

Energy on a swing 🛝

Think of a child on a swing. At the top of the swing she's momentarily still, but full of stored height. At the bottom she zooms along, full of motion.

Cappy stores energy in an electric field (like the height). Indy stores it in a magnetic field (like the motion). The energy slooooshes back and forth between them, over and over.

Real wires have a little resistance, so the swing slowly runs out of steam. That's damping. 🍃

f = 1 / (2π × √(L × C))

Bigger L or C = a lazier swing (lower frequency).

🎛️ Make it swing!

Set damping to 0 for a swing that never stops. Crank it up and it fades fast.

Why mirror images? Walking once around the loop, the voltage "drops" must add up to zero (that's Kirchhoff's voltage law 🕵️). So Indy's voltage mirrors Cappy's. With damping, the sneaky resistance takes a small share too.
